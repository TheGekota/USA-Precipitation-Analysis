# Key Metrics and Insights

Detailed findings from the USA May Runoff Trend Analysis (2000–2025). See [README.md](README.md) for the overview and [METHODOLOGY.md](METHODOLOGY.md) for the workflow.

## Output layers

Each value below is the range of cell values in that layer across the continental USA. They are not national averages.

| Layer | Cell values | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mean | 0–769 mm | Highest on the NW and NE coasts and scattered through the Rockies; lowest in the interior |
| Standard deviation | 0–1,491 mm | Highest on the NW and NE coasts and through the Rockies; moderate in the East South Central and South Atlantic; near zero inland |
| Minimum | 0–476 mm | Some cells never dropped below 476 mm in any May |
| Maximum | 0–4,747 mm | Very high maximums appear in the Rocky Mountain area as well as the coasts |
| Range | 0–4,709 mm | Largest in the Northwest, Northeast, and lower Southeast |

## Sample locations

| Location | Region | Typical May runoff | Notable events |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portland, OR | West Coast | ~50–150 mm | Spikes around 2005 and 2017 |
| Boston, MA | East Coast | ~25–140 mm | High year-to-year variation |
| Pierre, SD | Inland Midwest | ~0–25 mm | Consistently low |

| Gallatin National Forest, MT | Rocky Mountains | Low most years | About 650 mm in 2018 |

## Insights

### 1. Coasts are wettest and most variable
The Northwest and Northeast coasts have both the highest average May runoff and the most year-to-year variation. Proximity to the ocean, higher moisture, and spring snow and rain are likely contributors.

### 2. The interior is dry and predictable
Inland locations like Pierre, SD stay low and steady. Low variability makes these areas predictable, but consistently low runoff also points to limited surface water.

### 3. The Rockies are the most surprising region
Gallatin National Forest usually sits between the coastal and inland locations, but reached about 650 mm in 2018. Because May is snowmelt season in the mountains, heavy snowpack melting is a likely explanation, possibly with flooding.

### 4. Trends point in different directions by region
The slope layer shows declining May runoff along the Northeast coast and parts of the Northwest coast, and increases in the Rocky Mountains (including parts of Colorado and Utah, which haven't historically seen much runoff) and the East South Central region. Most of the interior shows little change.

### 5. Spikes tend to happen in the same years
Several locations spike around the same years, especially the mid-2000s and 2017–2018. This suggests large-scale weather systems are behind the extremes rather than purely local conditions.

## Caveats

These findings come from one month per year over 26 years, using linear trends without significance testing. Treat the trend directions as patterns worth investigating, not confirmed long-term change. See the Limitations section of the README.
